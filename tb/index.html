The Terebinth Translation of the New Testament

The Terebinth Translation aims to be a syntax-for-syntax translation, reliably passing the syntax of the Greek through into English whenever possible. It is called “Terebinth” because it is strategically “wooden” in form.

It is not designed to be read cover-to-cover as the most fluid, idiomatic translation, but to be consulted verse by verse for insight into the underlying syntax—most valuably by students of Greek, who can more easily see the grammar beneath the English. The translation is thus not meant to be a source of “meaning” but a transmitter of the grammar. “Helper” words not present in the Greek (itself often a difficult distinction to make) are included [in brackets].

This project is living: this version does not claim to be final, but is the first word in an ongoing conversation and improvement. Any errors are entirely my own. The Greek text is sourced from the SBLGNT for its availability; this is not a claim that it is the most authoritative Greek text.

— Timothy Noe
